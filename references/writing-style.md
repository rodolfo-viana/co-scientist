# Writing style

Adapted from the `WRITING_STYLE` rules in OpenScientist
(https://github.com/openscientist-io/openscientist, Apache License 2.0).
Changes: shortened, examples moved from biomedicine to ML.

These rules apply to every file a scientist reads: hypotheses, reviews,
rationales, plans, results and overviews.

- Punctuate with commas, colons, parentheses and full stops. Do not use the em
  dash, or an en dash or double hyphen in its place. Use straight quotes only.
- Say what a thing is or what the data shows. Do not frame a statement against
  a claim nobody made ("it is not X, it is Y").
- Prefer plain verbs: "is", "has", "shows", "uses".
- Choose the exact word over the impressive one. Avoid delve, crucial, pivotal,
  intricate, multifaceted, nuanced, robust (outside its statistical meaning),
  comprehensive, seamless, landscape, realm, tapestry, testament, underscore,
  showcase, foster, leverage, harness, garner, notably.
- Be specific. Give numbers, effect sizes, seeds, dataset and model names, and
  citation keys in place of "various", "several studies" or "recent years".
- Report significance only as far as the evidence goes.
- Use a list only for items that are genuinely separate. Do not group things in
  threes by habit, and do not start every bullet with a bold label.
- Start with the answer and stop when it is complete.
- State uncertainty once, where it applies, with its reason.
- Keep sentences short and vary their length. Write headings in sentence case.
